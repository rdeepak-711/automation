Planning Your Visit to Sagrada Família

Everything you need to know before you go.

Opening Hours

Sagrada Família is open daily with seasonal hours ranging from 9 AM–6 PM in winter to 9 AM–8 PM in summer. Last entry is 30 minutes before closing.

Know more →

Best Time to Visit

Early mornings offer the smallest crowds and magical light through the east stained glass. Late afternoon brings warm golden tones. Weekdays and winter months are quietest.

Know more →

Getting There

Take Metro L2 or L5 to Sagrada Família station — the exit leads right to the basilica. Buses 19, 33, 34, 43, 44, and 50 also stop nearby.

Know more →

Entrances

Two entrances serve visitors — Nativity Façade and Passion Façade. Ticket holders use Nativity Façade. Arrive 15 minutes early. Security screening and bag checks required.

Know more →

Dress Code

As an active place of worship, shoulders and knees must be covered. No swimwear, transparent clothes, or hats. Non-compliant visitors may be denied entry.

Know more →

Amenities

Restrooms, baby changing facilities, a gift shop, and refreshment areas are available. The basilica interior is wheelchair accessible, though towers are not.

Things to Know Before You Book

Practical tips to make the most of your visit.

🎫 Book online in advance — Popular time slots sell out days ahead, especially April–October. Always book online to guarantee your preferred time.
👗 Dress code enforced — Shoulders and knees must be covered. No swimwear, transparent clothes, or hats inside the basilica.
⏰ Arrive 15 minutes early — Entry is at your booked time slot only. Latecomers are not admitted and no refunds are provided.
🗼 Tower access is separate — Tower visits require an add-on. They are not wheelchair accessible — access is via elevator up and spiral stairs down.
📸 Photography allowed — Personal photography is permitted inside. Tripods, flash, and commercial photography require advance permission.
🔄 Cancellation policy — Most tickets are non-refundable. Some offer free cancellation up to 24 hours before — check the policy before booking.

What to See at Sagrada Família

Don't miss these highlights during your visit.

The Nativity Façade

The only façade completed in Gaudí's lifetime, depicting the birth of Jesus with incredibly detailed stone carvings of plants, animals, and celestial bodies. A UNESCO World Heritage highlight.

Know more →

Tower Views

Ascend the Nativity or Passion towers by elevator for breathtaking panoramic views of Barcelona, the Mediterranean, and Montjuïc. The spiral staircases down are an experience in themselves.

Know more →

The Interior & Stained Glass

Gaudí designed the interior as a forest of branching columns bathed in rainbow light from floor-to-ceiling stained glass. Morning brings warm tones; afternoon brings cool blues and greens.

Know more →

The Passion Façade

Sculptor Josep Maria Subirachs' angular, austere depiction of Christ's suffering. A dramatic contrast to the ornate Nativity Façade, full of hidden symbols and numerological puzzles.

Know more →

Gaudí's Tomb & Museum

The basement crypt houses Gaudí's tomb and a museum with original models, drawings, and photographs showing the basilica's evolution over 140+ years of construction.

Know more →

The Glory Façade

Still under construction, this will become the basilica's main entrance and largest façade. It will depict humanity's path to God, including death, final judgment, and celestial glory.
